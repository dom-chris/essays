Why the double-slit experiment mattered

A simple visual intuition for the problem that made quantum mechanics so strange.

SourceTwo slitsScreen

Classical expectation

If tiny objects were only little particles, we would expect each one to go through one slit or the other and land behind it. Repeated many times, the impacts would build up into two bands on the screen.

What to noticeEvery impact is local, and the final pattern simply reflects the two openings.
Where is pilot-wave theory? Deliberately not here. At this point in the essay the reader only needs the phenomenon: classical particles suggest two bands; waves produce interference; quantum objects arrive one by one yet build an interference pattern. The de Broglie–Bohm interpretation belongs later, once Bohm and the pilot-wave idea have been introduced.

Small glossary

Interference

A pattern produced when waves overlap and strengthen or weaken one another.

Wave-particle duality

The discovery that light and matter cannot be described adequately by the old simple opposition between “wave” and “particle”.

Why is the quantum case strange?

The detections are individual and localized, yet many detections build a pattern associated with wave interference. The experiment therefore forces a distinction between what is observed at the screen and whatever description we give of the quantum object before detection.
